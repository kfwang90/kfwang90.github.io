MIT: Randomly connected neural units self-assemble gradient-learning motifs matching backprop performance ⭐️ 8.0/10

MIT News reports on a PNAS paper (published 9 October 2026) showing that randomly interconnected neuron-like units, governed by shared local plasticity rules, can self-assemble gradient-learning motifs and achieve pattern-recognition and image-classification performance comparable to backpropagation-trained networks. The work demonstrates a biologically plausible mechanism where learning machinery emerges from local rules rather than being hand-designed, though the authors caution that self-organization does not prove the brain uses this mechanism and the motivation is biological plausibility rather than replacing backpropagation in large-scale models.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/Dorian182 · Oct 15, 10:01

「Background」 The work builds on longstanding efforts to find biologically plausible alternatives to backpropagation, which requires non-local error signals that are difficult to map onto known synaptic plasticity mechanisms. Earlier research on local learning rules, such as equilibrium propagation and predictive coding, showed that gradient-like learning can emerge from local updates, but typically assumed carefully designed architectures. This study instead demonstrates that the learning circuitry itself can self-assemble from random connectivity under shared plasticity rules, extending the principle of local credit assignment to the emergence of the learning machinery.

「Impact」 The result provides a concrete demonstration that gradient-based learning can emerge from local plasticity rules in randomly connected networks, offering a new research direction for neuromorphic computing and biologically plausible credit assignment. Researchers should evaluate motif stability across initial connectivity conditions and task generalization beyond the demonstrated classification benchmarks.

Scaling Kimi K3 on AMD MI355X: Matching B300 Throughput ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · vLLM Blog · Oct 16, 00:00

「Background」 AMD and vLLM collaborators optimized Kimi K3, a 93-layer model mixing 24 MLA attention layers (growing KV cache) with 69 KDA recurrent layers (fixed state), on an eight-GPU AMD Instinct MI355X system. The AgentX benchmark replays real coding-agent traces with high prefix reuse. At 50 tokens/s/user P90 interactivity, MI355X achieved 7,567 total tokens/s/GPU versus 6,083 for NVIDIA HGX B300 (24.4% higher), translating to 87.4% more tokens per dollar under SemiAnalysis TCO estimates ($1.50 vs $2.26/GPU-hour). Both GPUs share 288 GB HBM3E and 8 TB/s bandwidth, though B300 has higher dense matrix peaks (13.5 PFLOP/s FP4 vs 10.1 PFLOP/s MXFP4).

「Solution」 Six kernel-level optimizations closed the gap. (1) Decode Context Parallelism (DCP8) splits MLA history across GPUs, cutting per-GPU cache from ~1.96 GB to ~245 MB for a 142K-token conversation; CPU offload preserves reusable state in host DRAM with aligned MLA/KDA resume points. (2) MoE output projection deduplication assigns each GPU its output coordinates, using the existing shared-expert all-reduce to assemble the full result—eliminating 8× redundant projections and freeing ~3.85 GiB/GPU for cache (3.05–4.91% throughput gain, up to 28.9% at high concurrency). (3) Kernel fusion removed intermediate copies: KDA decode fused three kernels into one (AttnRes+RMSNorm kept mixtures in FP32 registers), and the GEMM dispatcher stopped packing unused fallback inputs (69 copies/step, 0.339 ms saved). (4) Padding MLA query heads from 12 to 16 unlocked AMD AITER's assembly decode path, halving long-context decode latency (43.8→23.6 ms/token at 32K input). (5) KDA prefill/verification parallelization: fused prefill computes group transformations before state arrives, then an ordered scan assigns starting states for parallel replay; speculative verification switched to 4-column tiles, raising workgroups from 48 to 384 and delivering 2.89× verification speedup (16.2→5.61 µs/layer). (6) Speculative decoding economics: with AgentX's fixed acceptance, 4 drafts yield 12% more committed tokens per cycle than 3, so the full cycle must grow <12% to justify the extra draft; the recipe uses 3–7 drafts at DCP1 but disables speculation at DCP8.

「Takeaway」 Matching NVIDIA's throughput on competitive hardware required eliminating redundant work across the stack—duplicate cache storage, repeated MoE projections, unnecessary kernel handoffs, and underutilized compute units—rather than chasing peak FLOPs. The optimizations demonstrate that inference efficiency comes from aligning serving decisions (cache policy, parallelism strategy, draft depth) with kernel scheduling and memory hierarchy, and that the same principles (compare ranks, follow buffers, count independent work) transfer to other models and workloads.
